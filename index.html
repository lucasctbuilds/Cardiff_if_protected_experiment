    How this works

    A self-check and design brief, not a grade. Each building gets a 0–1 score on seven future values from the project's research brief (data/future-model/future-value-research.md, A1–A7), weighted as the 1947–2026 evidence suggests: Social ×3.0, Adaptability ×1.8, Living economy ×1.2, Climate ×1.0, Carbon ×0.5, Natural capital ×0.4, Recognition ×0.3 (C3), plus an Economy × Adaptability term. A building scoring the median on everything gets 47.5%, the share of Cardiff's 1947 buildings still standing about 79 years later. The band (A ≥70%, B 55–70%, C 40–55%, D 25–40%, E under 25%) is the Continuity scenario; the range is across four scenarios (C4). Moving the building changes homes and community buildings nearby, station distance, green space and the river; the card lists them under "Here". The Embankment (21/00783/MJR) is set aside: our project tests its own proposals for the site, so the masterplan is excluded from every input.

    Limits

    Indicative only: the weights are priors, not fitted to the future, and the Economy × Adaptability weight is a placeholder. Real buildings where they stand are scored on the project's 1947 records. Homes today are estimated from OpenStreetMap tags; planned homes are shared over the schemes' drawn blocks. Flood exposure is known only as a 200 m box at the site, so it is the same everywhere in the area. Daylight is flagged, not scored. Buildings from outside Cardiff are judged with Cardiff's 1947 evidence. Inputs the project cannot fill are "not known" and count as the median.
